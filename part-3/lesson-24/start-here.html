New User? Start Here

If this is the first time you visit this site, you may be wondering how it can help you become a better programmer. On this page I explain why I created this site and what benefits you can get as a hobbyist or professional developer.

Who Am I?

My name is Salvatore D'Angelo and I am a professional software engineer working for a well-known American software company. You can read my story here.

What Is My Experience?

With over 30 years of experience as a professional software developer, I have had the opportunity to work on numerous projects. You can read my full career on my LinkedIn page. I have been involved in all phases of the software development lifecycle.

Why This Website?

I love writing — I think it is a fantastic tool for consolidating knowledge. For this reason I decided to open this site to share everything I have learned over the years. I believe it can help other people who want to enter this amazing world, both professionally and as a hobby.

I have always believed that the best way to learn a new programming language or a new technology is to build a small project with it.

This will be the philosophy of this site: explain how to use programming languages and technologies through small projects or step-by-step tutorials.

This philosophy and my perspective on the software development world are a good mix to help you understand how to learn a language or technology, what truly matters, and how companies today face the new challenges the world poses.

How to Get Started?

This site contains a series of projects where I explain, step by step, how to use a specific technology. I recommend visiting the Projects page and finding a project that interests you.

In all these years of experience, I have learned that people learn faster when they study something they are passionate about.

All projects and tutorials provide source code that you can download from my GitHub page.

Once you have selected a project, read the articles and try to understand the basic concepts. At this stage it is not important that you understand every part of the source code. When the basic concepts are clear, download the source code and start experimenting.

Once you are able to run the software, you can go deeper and analyse how it works. At this stage you can fork the projects and try to fix bugs or add new features. Feel free to do so and open a pull request — I will be happy to merge your contribution into the original code.

On my blog there are also some standalone articles that are not part of any project. You can find them on the Blog page by following the links at the bottom of each page.
